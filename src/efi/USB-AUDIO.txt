CHARON: SPEECH THROUGH USB SOUND DEVICES BEFORE THE SYSTEM STARTS
Written 2026-11-22 for r407. Replaces the r405 notes at the head of CharonUsbAudio.h.

This file says what Charon's USB Audio Class output handles, by which rules,
where each rule comes from, what the USB host controller question looks like,
what stays impossible, and how each point was checked. It is a capability
description, NOT a hardware certification: the only device the code ever ran
against is QEMU's emulated usb-audio (one USB Audio Class 1.0 device, full
speed, synchronous endpoint) under OVMF/edk2. No physical sound card, headset,
USB-C adapter, dock or physical computer was used. Section 8 lists exactly
which parts were run and which are written from the specifications only.

Files
  CharonUsbAudio.h          the device side: USB I/O requests, the xHCI endpoint
                            rings, hot-plug, the set of devices (UsbAudioSet)
  CharonUsbAudioModel.h     the pure logic: descriptor parser, pacing, feedback,
                            sample conversion, the two transfer rings
  CharonUsbAudioQuirks.h    GENERATED device quirk table (tools/build-usb-audio-quirks.py)
  tests/CharonUsbAudioTests.cpp   host tests of the pure logic
  CharonAudioDevice.h       chooses among HD Audio, AC'97, ES1371 and USB audio

1. THE BUS QUESTION FIRST: WHO SENDS THE ISOCHRONOUS PACKETS

A USB sound device takes its samples through an isochronous endpoint. UEFI
defines isochronous transfers in two protocols:
  EFI_USB_IO_PROTOCOL       UsbIsochronousTransfer(This, DeviceEndpoint, Data,
                            DataLength, Status) and UsbAsyncIsochronousTransfer
                            (the same plus a callback)
  EFI_USB2_HC_PROTOCOL      IsochronousTransfer(This, DeviceAddress,
                            EndPointAddress, DeviceSpeed, MaximumPacketLength,
                            DataBuffersNumber, Data[], DataLength, Translator,
                            TransferResult) and AsyncIsochronousTransfer
Neither form lets the caller give the length of each packet: only a buffer, its
length and (host controller form) a maximum packet length. A stream at 44.1 kHz
(packets of 44 and 45 frames) or one that follows a feedback endpoint cannot be
expressed, and a synchronous call cannot be chained without a gap.

What the reference firmware does (edk2 master, files read on 2026-11-22, all
"SPDX-License-Identifier: BSD-2-Clause-Patent"; nothing copied from them):
  MdeModulePkg/Bus/Usb/UsbBusDxe/UsbBus.c
      UsbIoIsochronousTransfer, UsbIoAsyncIsochronousTransfer: the whole body is
      "return EFI_UNSUPPORTED;"
  MdeModulePkg/Bus/Pci/XhciDxe/Xhci.c
      XhcIsochronousTransfer, XhcAsyncIsochronousTransfer: "return EFI_UNSUPPORTED;"
  MdeModulePkg/Bus/Pci/XhciDxe/XhciSched.c
      XhcInitializeEndpointContext (both context sizes): isochronous endpoints
      are skipped, "Do not support isochronous transfer now. ... Transfer ring is
      not allocated."
  MdeModulePkg/Bus/Pci/EhciDxe/Ehci.c
      EhcIsochronousTransfer, EhcAsyncIsochronousTransfer: "return EFI_UNSUPPORTED;"
  MdeModulePkg/Bus/Pci/UhciDxe/Uhci.c
      Uhci2IsochronousTransfer, Uhci2AsyncIsochronousTransfer: "return EFI_UNSUPPORTED;"
  OHCI: MdeModulePkg/Bus/Pci holds EhciDxe, UhciDxe and XhciDxe only (directory
      listing of 2026-11-22); edk2 master has no OHCI driver at all.
  https://github.com/tianocore/edk2/tree/master/MdeModulePkg/Bus
So firmware that uses edk2's own USB drivers (OVMF as tested here; any build
that takes these MdeModulePkg drivers unchanged) cannot play one sample through
its USB stack, on any controller type. Whether the makers' own USB stacks (AMI,
Insyde, Phoenix, Apple) differ is not established - no public documentation was
found (section 9) - and Charon does not depend on it either way: it never calls
the isochronous transfer functions.

What Charon does (since r405, reviewed and widened in r407): it does NOT own the
controller and does not ask the firmware for isochronous transfers. On an xHCI
controller it drives the audio endpoints itself BESIDE the firmware's driver,
which keeps the controller, its command ring, its event ring and every other
device - the USB keyboard on the same controller included:
  * descriptors, SET_INTERFACE and all class requests (rate, clock, mute,
    volume, selector) go through EFI_USB_IO_PROTOCOL.UsbControlTransfer;
  * one Configure Endpoint command is run in a borrowed slot of the firmware's
    command ring with interrupts off, and the ring is put back byte for byte;
  * the endpoints' transfer events go to a spare interrupter (an event ring the
    firmware does not use) with its interrupt left disabled;
  * the endpoints' doorbells are rung.
The head of CharonUsbAudio.h describes the mechanism; every step is checked
and anything unexpected ends in a refusal that leaves the controller as found.

Controller types
  xHCI   SUPPORTED as described. Every USB 3 port, every USB-C port, and the USB 2
         ports of PCs built since about 2013-2015 hang on an xHCI controller
         (the later date is an inference from chipset generations, not a
         measurement here). Full-speed, high-speed and SuperSpeed devices,
         directly or behind hubs and docks (the device is found by its root
         port and route string).
  EHCI   NOT SUPPORTED. The driver refuses and says so in the trace
  UHCI   ("CHARON usb audio on a controller that is not xHCI, programming
  OHCI   interface=.."). See section 6 for what a fallback would take.

2. WHAT IS SUPPORTED

Classes
  USB Audio Class 1.0   AudioStreaming interfaces with a Type I format
                        descriptor; rate by the endpoint's sampling frequency
                        control.
  USB Audio Class 2.0   Type I formats; rate by the clock source entity (range
                        request, set, read back); clock selectors and
                        multipliers on the way; clock validity.
  USB Audio Class 3.0   both forms: the Basic Audio Device Definition profiles
                        (no class descriptors: 48 kHz, format inferred from
                        wMaxPacketSize, entities with fixed numbers) and full
                        class descriptors (channel count from the cluster
                        descriptor, a high capability descriptor request).
  Configurations        The configuration the firmware selected is used and
                        never changed. edk2 selects the first one
                        (UsbBusDxe/UsbEnumer.c: Configs[0]); a UAC 3.0 device
                        offers its backwards compatible UAC 1.0/2.0
                        configuration there, so it normally plays as a UAC 1.0
                        or 2.0 device. If firmware selected the UAC 3.0
                        configuration instead, that one is played.
                        SET_CONFIGURATION is deliberately not sent: edk2 then
                        removes and re-creates every interface handle of the
                        device (UsbBus.c, UsbIoControlTransfer), the HID keys
                        of a headset among them.
Formats
  PCM 16, 24 and 32 bit (2, 3 or 4 bytes per sample, the 16 bits of the voice
  in the most significant bytes), preferred in that order. Only when a device
  has nothing else: 8-bit PCM (signed in a PCM format, unsigned as "PCM8") and
  IEEE 754 single precision. 1 to 32 channels: mono gets the mean of Charon's
  two channels, more channels get left and right in the first two and silence
  in the rest. Not handled: Type II/III formats (compressed, IEC 61937), DSD,
  big-endian samples of a few old devices, 64-bit float.
Rates
  Any rate from 8 kHz to 768 kHz that fits the endpoint. Order of choice:
  48000 (Charon's own rate, no conversion), 44100, then the nearest to 48000.
  Conversion from Charon's 48 kHz stereo mix (the same PCM the HD Audio, AC'97
  and ES1371 outputs get) is linear interpolation; below 44.1 kHz two one-pole
  low-pass stages at 0.42 of the device rate run in front of it, so 8 and 16 kHz
  headsets do not get the upper speech band folded down.
Speeds and packets
  Full speed (1 ms frames), high speed and SuperSpeed (bInterval 1..7, 125 us to
  8 ms). One packet per service interval, its length exact: rate * interval,
  spread without drift (44.1 kHz at 1 ms: nine packets of 44 frames, one of
  45). High-bandwidth high-speed endpoints (up to three transactions per
  microframe) and SuperSpeed bursts (endpoint companion descriptor: burst,
  mult, bytes per interval) are declared to the controller and each packet's
  burst counts are filled in.
Synchronisation
  synchronous, adaptive   the nominal rate, exact.
  asynchronous with a     the feedback endpoint is read through a second ring;
  feedback endpoint       the value's fixed-point format (10.14 in 3 bytes,
                          16.16 in 4 bytes, and devices that send the other one)
                          is recognised by size; packets 5 ms and more ahead of
                          the controller get the lengths the device asks for,
                          silence too, so the device's buffer stays level. A
                          value outside -1/8..+1/2 of the nominal rate is
                          ignored; no value for a second: nominal rate again.
                          If the controller refuses the second endpoint the
                          stream starts without it.
  asynchronous without    (implicit feedback: the device expects the host to
  one                     copy the recording endpoint's packet sizes) the
                          nominal rate is sent and the trace says so. Such a
                          device plays, with a click whenever its buffer has
                          drifted full or empty. Running the recording stream
                          as a clock reference is not implemented.
Controls
  Every output terminal the stream reaches is opened: feature units on the way
  are unmuted (master and each channel) and their volume set to 0 dB, or to the
  maximum when the range ends below 0 dB (the range is read; a missing or
  inverted range leaves the device's own setting). Selector units on the way
  are switched to the input that carries the stream. Mixer, processing,
  effect and extension units are passed through but not programmed (their
  defaults stay). Charon's own volume setting acts on the mix as for every card.
Composite devices
  Only the playback AudioStreaming interface is touched (its alternate setting,
  its endpoint, the AudioControl requests named above). The microphone
  interface, HID buttons, a hub or other functions of the same device are not
  opened and get no request.

3. WHICH DEVICE SPEAKS

A dock's empty headphone jack and a headset both describe themselves as
"headphones", so no rule can tell which of several devices is at the user's
ears. Therefore: EVERY playable USB sound interface plays, four at the most, in
the order the firmware lists them, each named in the trace
("CHARON usb audio plays on=<rank><vendor><product>"). One of them paces the
speech - the lowest output terminal rank (headphones and headsets, then
speakers and speakerphones, then undefined, then line connectors, then S/PDIF
and other digital links), the first of equals; "CHARON usb audio pacing
device" names it. The others get a copy.
Beside a built-in card that plays (HD Audio, AC'97, ES1371) the USB devices get
a copy of everything the built-in card accepted (r405 "mirror"), so a laptop
whose own speakers stay silent still speaks through a USB device. Without a
usable built-in card the USB devices are the only output.

4. HOT-PLUG

  Pulled out    The stream's timer notices within 160 ms (USB I/O instance gone,
                slot gone, controller error) and stops touching the device.
                The endpoint is removed from the controller, memory and the
                spare interrupter are given back at the next call from the sound
                path. Other outputs go on; when it was the only output the voice
                provider looks for a card twelve times and then only now and
                then (CharonVoiceProvider.h), so there is no flood.
  Plugged in    While a card plays, the sound path counts the USB I/O handles
                once per 32 calls (no allocation) and scans only when the count
                changed. A new device is started and plays from then on. When
                there was no output at all, the provider's next attempt finds
                it; r407 adds the handle count as a cue, so the wait of the
                throttled retries is skipped.
  Limit         All of this runs from the sound path. While the reader is
                silent nothing looks at the bus: a device plugged in then is
                taken at the next utterance, whose first words may be late.

5. DEVICE QUIRKS

CharonUsbAudioQuirks.h is generated by tools/build-usb-audio-quirks.py from
Linux v6.12:
  sound/usb/quirks.c    quirk_flags_table[]            114 entries read
  sound/usb/implicit.c  playback_implicit_fb_quirks[]   13 device entries read
Both files carry "SPDX-License-Identifier: GPL-2.0-or-later" (the generator
checks the line and stops otherwise); Charon's own parts are GPL-2.0-or-later.
Only data is taken (USB IDs and flag names), no code. 81 entries are kept;
entries whose flags concern only suspend, DSD or the media controller are
dropped. Matching is the kernel's: the device's own entry, else its vendor's.
  Acted on:  no rate read-back; 20 / 5 / 1 ms pause after each class request;
             50 ms after SET_INTERFACE; SET_INTERFACE before the clock (UAC 2/3);
             alternate setting 0 first; rate not set when there is one;
             clock selector never written; hard-wired clock accepted.
  Recorded:  implicit feedback devices (they play at the nominal rate, see 2).
  Refused:   packets with a length header (QUIRK_FLAG_TX_LENGTH, two Zoom
             recorders): not implemented.
  NOT imported: the kernel's device-specific code - boot quirks (vendor
             requests that switch a device into audio mode: Creative Extigy,
             M-Audio Fast Track, Digidesign Mbox, Native Instruments, Axe-Fx
             and others), format fix-ups, and the descriptor table of
             quirks-table.h for devices that do not declare the audio class
             (old Roland/Edirol/Yamaha interfaces with vendor-specific
             interfaces). Those devices are not handled.
Kernel files only read for facts, nothing taken (licence noted because they are
NOT "or later"): include/linux/usb/audio-v2.h (GPL-2.0), sound/usb/card.h
(GPL-2.0), drivers/usb/core/generic.c (GPL-2.0),
include/uapi/linux/usb/audio.h (GPL-2.0 WITH Linux-syscall-note). Read and
compatible: sound/usb/endpoint.c, clock.c, format.c, stream.c, mixer.c, pcm.c,
card.c, helper.c, usbaudio.h, quirks-table.h (GPL-2.0-or-later),
include/linux/usb/audio-v3.h (GPL-2.0+). One method follows the kernel
(endpoint.c, snd_usb_handle_sync_urb): recognising a feedback value's format by
shifting it until it matches the nominal rate; it is written anew in
CharonUsbAudioModel.h and marked there.

6. WHAT REMAINS IMPOSSIBLE OR OPEN, AND WHY

EHCI, UHCI, OHCI controllers (computers from before USB 3, some USB 2-only
ports of older boards). The firmware's drivers have no isochronous transfers
(section 1) and Charon has no ring of its own to put beside them: these
controllers have ONE periodic schedule (the frame list), owned by the firmware
driver, and the keyboard's interrupt transfers live in it.
  Owning the controller (halting it, installing Charon's own schedule) would
  take the keyboard and the boot stick away from the firmware: not done, and
  not planned.
  A fallback beside the firmware driver is conceivable for two of the three and
  is the next step if such machines matter:
    UHCI  link one isochronous TD per frame in front of the firmware's queue
          heads in each frame list entry; TDs complete in place.
    EHCI  the same with iTDs for high-speed devices; full-speed devices sit
          behind a transaction translator and need split-transaction siTDs
          (start-splits of at most 188 bytes per microframe).
    OHCI  no: finished isochronous TDs go to the controller's single done
          queue, which the firmware driver empties; its code would meet TDs it
          never made.
  Open problems of that design: (1) the device's bus address is known only
  inside the firmware's bus driver - it would have to be found by sending
  GET_DESCRIPTOR/GET_INTERFACE to each address through EFI_USB2_HC_PROTOCOL and
  matching the answers; (2) the firmware driver rewrites a frame list entry
  when an interrupt endpoint is added (a keyboard plugged in), dropping
  Charon's link until it is put back; (3) QEMU can exercise the UHCI case with
  its usb-audio device, but not EHCI (the emulated device is full speed and the
  emulated hub is USB 1.1), so half of it could not be checked here. It was not
  started: outside the budget of this batch and not safe to ship unchecked.
  Whoever has such a machine: its built-in sound is normally plain HD Audio or
  AC'97, which Charon plays directly.
xHCI controllers Charon refuses: no spare interrupter (controller with a
  single one), the command ring's position not recognisable, device memory
  addresses translated by an IOMMU, a firmware driver that is not running the
  controller. The trace names the step ("identify", "attach" with the code).
Before the firmware has connected its USB drivers (very early Driver####
  start) there is no EFI_USB_IO instance yet; the device is taken when one
  appears.
After ExitBootServices nothing plays (section 7).
Bluetooth headsets, wireless headsets whose dongle is not an audio class
  device, and sound over HDMI/DisplayPort of a dock are not USB audio class
  playback and are not covered.
Jack detection: a dock's or adapter's empty jack cannot be told from a used
  one (UAC 2.0/3.0 connector controls are not read); playing on every device
  (section 3) is the answer instead.

7. EXITBOOTSERVICES (changed in r407)

Before r407 the exit notification removed the endpoint with a command on the
firmware's command ring and waited with Stall for its completion - inside the
notification, and with the endpoint left pointing into memory the system was
about to reuse if the command did not complete. Now the notification runs no
command, calls no Stall, takes no memory map and frees nothing:
  1. both rings are cut short behind the two TDs the controller may already
     hold (the cycle bit of every later TD is flipped: memory writes only);
  2. the controller's frame counter (MFINDEX) is read until three service
     intervals have passed - register reads, bounded by a count, 3 ms for a
     full-speed device (this replaces the Stall: the last TDs must be over
     before their event ring goes away);
  3. the spare interrupter is switched off, so the controller writes nothing
     into Charon's memory any more.
The endpoint contexts still name ring memory, but the controller has found the
end of both rings and reads no further; an operating system resets the
controller when it takes it over. After the exit signal the object refuses to
open again and Stop frees nothing. NOT exercised in a test: no run here handed
over to an operating system with the USB stream open.

8. HOW EACH POINT WAS CHECKED

Host tests (tests/CharonUsbAudioTests.cpp, built with clang++ -Wall -Wextra,
"PASS CharonUsbAudioTests" on 2026-11-22). They run the pure logic against
hand-written descriptors and a small model of an xHCI endpoint - no device:
  r405 tests kept     UAC 1.0 speaker and headset, UAC 2.0 DAC, pacing, ring
                      laps, lead limit, Discard, lost events, 44.1 and 96 kHz
                      conversion, mono and six channels, event ring position.
  new in r407         UAC 2.0 high-speed asynchronous headset with feedback
                      endpoint, selector unit, clock selector with two sources,
                      two output terminals, high-bandwidth endpoint; UAC 1.0
                      asynchronous endpoint with synch endpoint; 8-bit and
                      float settings and their order of choice; BADD profile
                      (stereo 16, mono 24, wrong size, microphone interface);
                      full UAC 3.0 descriptors on SuperSpeed with companion
                      descriptor; truncation and single-byte corruption of all
                      of them (no read outside, no hang); feedback values
                      (10.14, 16.16 per microframe, per frame, out of range);
                      a ring following 48.25 frames per packet for two seconds
                      with the speech intact, and back to nominal; 8 kHz at
                      125 us with empty packets; unsigned and signed 8-bit,
                      float; the 16 kHz low-pass; burst counts; the feedback
                      ring; quirk lookup.
Builds (tools/build-charon-windows.py, -Wall -Wextra -Werror, 2026-11-22):
  work/charon-win-r407usb1, -usb1-drv   first state (run 1)
  work/charon-win-r407usb2, -usb2-drv   with the plug-in cue (runs 2 and 3)
  work/charon-win-r407usb3, -usb3-drv   final state (run 4): packet lengths
                                        capped at the endpoint's capacity.
  CHARON.EFI and CHARONVOICE.EFI link in all three.
QEMU runs (q35 + OVMF, TCG, qemu-xhci, usb-audio and usb-kbd on the same
controller, host sound through DirectSound and recorded with the monitor's
wavcapture; trace on the serial port). CHARON.EFI only; the driver image was
built, not run.
  Run 1 (usb1 build; no built-in card, CHARON_SOUNDCARD=none)
    Trace: identify (slot 2, full speed), device 46f4:0002, format 48000 Hz
    2 channels 16 bit UAC 1.0 alternate 1, endpoint 0x01 attributes 0x0d
    192 bytes, output terminal 0x0301 with one feature unit, volume 0 dB of
    -128..+8 dB on channels 1 and 2, attach on interrupter 15, first events,
    "plays on", "primary=0", "first PCM".
    Recorded sound: 65 seconds with sound out of 208 recorded (rms above 50 of
    32768), peak 18203 - the machine has no other sound device.
    Keyboard: "help" and "ver" typed on the USB keyboard reached the firmware
    shell while the stream ran and after the unplug (screen dump).
    Unplug (device_del): "vanished", "stopped=0"; the provider tried 12 times
    and then once in the next 8 s. Plug-in (device_add): found, "audio
    reopened", sound recorded again (13 of the following seconds).
    Finding: after the twelve tries were used up, the voice came back about 8 s
    after the first key - the throttled retry. Fixed by the handle-count cue.
  Run 2 (usb2 build, same machine)
    Unplug: released 3.1 s later (at the first utterance), 12 tries, one
    not-found line in the following 10 s. Plug-in: keys typed 4.0 s later, the
    device played 1.6 s after the first key (5.6 s after plug-in) with no
    failed attempt in between; sound recorded again.
  Run 3 (usb2 build, built-in HD Audio through AudioDxe plus xHCI)
    USB device plugged in while HD Audio played: started as the copy at the
    first utterance ("plays on"). Pulled out: "vanished", "unplugged",
    "stopped=0"; the built-in card went on without a fault or reopen.
  Run 4 (usb3 build = the final sources, machine of runs 1 and 2)
    Speech through the USB device: 40 seconds with sound of 47 recorded, peak
    17424, stream errors 0. Unplug: released 3.1 s later, 12 tries, one
    not-found line in the next 10 s. Plug-in: played again 1.6 s after the
    first key, no failed attempt in between.
  The four machines were stopped by their own process numbers and their
  folders deleted; the traces above are quoted from the serial logs, kept in
  tests/charon-r407-usb-audio-results.txt.

NOT EXERCISED BY ANY DEVICE (host tests of the logic only, or nothing):
  USB Audio Class 2.0 and 3.0 (parser, clock requests, cluster request);
  high speed and SuperSpeed (endpoint contexts with burst and mult, 125 us
  packets); devices behind a hub or dock; asynchronous endpoints: the feedback
  endpoint context, its ring, its events, and the rewriting of the length of
  queued TDs (xHCI controllers that read TRBs far ahead would send a packet
  with its old length now and then - no worse than without feedback, but
  unmeasured); 8-bit and float devices; rates other than 48 kHz on a device;
  several USB sound devices at once; selector units; the quirk pauses and
  orders; the ExitBootServices path; CHARONVOICE.EFI with USB audio; every
  firmware other than OVMF; every physical controller. The xHCI mechanism
  itself (borrowed command slot, spare interrupter) has only ever run on QEMU's
  emulated controller with edk2's driver.
