2. Cause
--------
2.1 Why the stream stalls in VMware at all - MEASURED in this work (vmware.log against the serial trace)
    The stream driver declares EFI_TIMEOUT when its 1 ms poll comes more than 70 ms late (HAK_RING_MAX_POLL_NS,
    HakAudioRing.h:11; 20 ms in the first revision, as BUILD-NOTES.md still says near its top) or the hardware
    cursor stands still for 200 ms (src/efi/audio_stream/driver/HakAudioStream.c:109-110, :125-126).
    [Corrected in section 6: this text first said 20 ms.]
    VMware creates the host's sound stream on the machine's processor thread when the guest starts the stream:
      vmware-new.log 14:40:27.018 vcpu-0 "Creating WAVE backend stream" .. 27.056 "Created poll thread" (38 ms);
      Charon's trace: first pump of the first utterance returns at 27.058, "pump device=...12" at 27.059.
    The guest does not run for those 38 ms and the poll is 38 ms late. On an idle host the creation takes
    23-48 ms (section 6), more than half of the 70 ms the driver allows; anything else that holds the machine
    in the same moment, or a busier host, takes it over the limit: the recoveries at start-up and the "pairs"
    (a recovery 25-35 ms after a recovery) are this. The driver takes its clock BEFORE it starts the stream
    (HakAudioStream.c:194-196: LastTick, then HdaControllerSetStreamState(TRUE)).
2.2 Every restart is paid for on the processor thread - MEASURED
    For each "CHARON audio recovered" vmware.log has, on vcpu-0: "Stopping WAVE stream", "Closing WAVE stream",
    "Destroying poll thread", "Creating WAVE backend stream", "SoundWaveSelectHostDevice", "Initializing 18
    buffers", "Created poll thread" (vmware-base.log: 67 creations for 65 recoveries). While those host calls
    run, no instruction of the guest runs: no key, no speech, no trace, no timeout of Charon's own.
2.3 The storm - MEASURED for the mechanism, INFERRED for the old trace
    The restart itself creates the condition of the next fault (2.1): whenever the host needs more than 70 ms
    to create its stream, the restarted stream faults at once, is restarted, faults ... Before this work
    nothing stopped that but a count of 64 restarts per utterance (CharonVoiceProvider.h, Recover(); was
    line 117, "recoveries_>=maximumRecoveries").
    With several machines playing at once and the host short of disk, the host's sound calls took seconds
    instead of 30 ms. Six restarts in about a minute, sound blips 2 to 8 s apart, then sound again: each
    restart held the machine for seconds, inside a step of the dialogue that reads no keys while it waits for
    the speech (HearOut). That is the "hang". INFERRED - it fits every number of section 1, but the machine's
    own log of that minute does not exist. It could NOT be reproduced as a host-side stall: making VMware's
    sound thread or the host's audio service stop needs administrator rights (OpenThread on vmware-vmx: error
    5), and stalling the host's audio would have disturbed the other agents' machines.
2.4 A second defect found on the way - MEASURED (reproduced, section 4.3)
    After the 64th restart of one utterance the old code never spoke again in that session: SpeakThunk asked
    EnsureRunning first, EnsureRunning went to Recover, Recover returned the fault because the count was
    used up, and the count is reset only AFTER a successful EnsureRunning (old lines 190-191). With a stall
    that lasts, the 64 restarts are used up in 1.75 s. From then on keys still move the dialogue on the screen,
    every cue is "deferred" and nothing is spoken: for a blind user, and for a test that watches the spoken
    lines of the trace, the dialogue is dead.
2.5 Checked and found bounded (no change needed)
    Every wait for speech in the dialogue code is a counted loop: CharonWsInstall.h:290 (HearOut),
    CharonWsAsk.h:233, CharonWsRamDisk.h:82/:396, CharonWsBrowser.h:228, BootMenu.h:732-736 (LaunchSay),
    :1503-1508, CharonResident.h:4386-4393, :4887-4893, :4929-4935. VoiceProvider::Stop drains for 5000 x 1 ms
    at most. AudioDxe: HdaControllerSendCommands (10 x 5 ms per verb, one retry), stream reset and run/stop
    (PollMem 100 ms / 10 ms), HakHdaCodecWaitPower (50 x 2 ms) are bounded; the driver's only unbounded loop
    (HdaCodecAudioIoStartPlayback, "while (StreamRunning)") is not on Charon's path. The AudioEngine and
    LiveVoice pumps are bounded by their budgets. The trace's serial output is bounded (1024 polls per byte).
    No unbounded wait on audio progress was found in Charon's own code; what was unbounded in effect was the
    NUMBER and RATE of device restarts, each of which the hypervisor may stretch without limit.


3. The fix (audio/voice files only; the dialogue headers were not touched)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  New: src/efi/CharonAudioStall.h - class StallPolicy, no EFI types (so the host test runs the same code).
    A stream that stalls again before it has taken 300 ms of sound did not recover. The third such stall in a
    row is not restarted: the stream stays stopped ("rests") 1 s, then 2, 4, at most 8 s while it keeps
    stalling, and is opened again after the rest; the stream opened after a rest gets one try. A restart that
    itself took more than 250 ms counts double and is followed by a rest of at least four times its own
    length (60 s at most). 4 s of sound without a restart ends the episode. Stalls with sound in between are
    restarted at once, as since r400.
  src/efi/CharonVoiceProvider.h
    :121-152 Recover(): asks the policy; on "rest" the stream is stopped and NOT opened; a restart that fails
             to open is followed by a rest instead of a failed voice; the 64-per-utterance count now leads to
             a rest, not to a dead voice (2.4).
    :159-181 Ticks()/CalibrateClock(): the processor's time-stamp counter, measured against Stall once in
             Initialize (:50; 12 ms at start-up - the only change on the normal path). Firmware timer events
             are not used: their time stands still while the machine is held.
    :185     Rest(): trace "CHARON audio stalled, stream rests, s=N", success for the caller.
    :190-194 EnsureRunning(): nothing touches the card during a rest; trace "CHARON audio rest over";
    :229-236 the open after a rest is timed, and a failed one leads to the next, longer rest.
    :301     PumpThunk returns success at once during a rest: speech stays queued (a newer utterance replaces
             it as always), no synthesis, no device call. So the dialogue's counted waits run at full speed
             and the workspace no longer latches "audio failed" (CharonWsCore.h:133) because of a stall.
    :344     the policy is told how much the stream has played.
    :377, :399, :459 cues, tones and beeps asked for during a rest are dropped (a cue seconds late says nothing).
    Traces added: "CHARON audio clock, ticks per ms", "... restart slow, ms", "... restart status",
             "... open after rest", "... open after rest slow, ms".
  src/efi/CharonAudioDevice.h:129 Submitted() (frames the HDA stream took since Open).
  Test-only code, compiled only with -DHAK_CHARON_STALLTEST (CharonVoiceProvider.h:786-796, :268, :345;
    CharonAudioDevice.h:130-132): for every utterance longer than 150 bytes the stream is treated as stalled
    from 1.5 s to 9.5 s after it was asked for, on every pump; the REAL stream is then stopped and restarted as
    after the driver's EFI_TIMEOUT. -DHAK_CHARON_STALLTEST_OLD (:133-137) switches the rule before r409 back
    on, to compare. Neither is defined in a normal build.
  One mistake made and removed on the way: the clock was first measured inside Recover with Stall. The screen
    reader hooks Stall and changes the synthesizer in it (CharonResident.h:4608-4614); in the one run where two
    start-up recoveries hit that path, speech came out in scraps for the rest of the session (serial-new.txt,
    meter-new.txt). The measurement was moved to Initialize; the cause of the scraps is INFERRED from that one
    run, the later runs with recoveries and the measurement in Initialize speak normally.

5. Not proven, and what remains
-------------------------------
  1. That the hang of 13:25 was the mechanism of 2.3. It is the reading that fits the meter and the trace; the
     machine's own log is gone and a host-side stall could not be made without administrator rights.
  2. A single restart or the driver's own stop that the hypervisor never lets return cannot be bounded by any
     code in the guest. The fix bounds how often Charon gives the host that chance (two restarts, then one
     try per rest) and how long it keeps trying; it cannot interrupt one call.
  3. The trigger itself is in the stream driver and is NOT fixed: AudioDxe takes its poll clock before it
     starts the stream (HakAudioStream.c:194-196), so the time the hypervisor needs to create its host stream
     (23-48 ms idle, measured) counts against the 70 ms limit. DONE LATER as a candidate driver, AudioDxe-r409:
     see section 6.
  4. During the dialogue's HearOut steps no key is read (up to 15 s, 30 s at some places). With speech that
     cannot be heard that is 15 s of a silent, deaf dialogue. A fix belongs in CharonWsInstall.h:289-291
     (another agent's file): leave the loop on any key press, and leave it early when the voice reports a
     rest - the provider would need one more effects call for that ("AudioResting"). Not done.
  5. While the stream rests, speech asked for is kept and spoken after the rest, but only the newest
     utterance: what the dialogue said in between is lost to the ear. On the screen and in braille it stays.
  6. Real hardware: nothing of this ran on a physical sound card. There a stall with sound in between (a disk
     read) takes the unchanged path; three stalls in a row without 300 ms of sound now cost a 1 s pause where
     they used to be restarted at once.
  7. The resident reader (CHARONVOICE.EFI as Driver####) uses the same provider; not run with this change.
  8. AC'97, ES1371 and USB sound devices never report EFI_TIMEOUT; their paths are unchanged and were not run.

6.0 Correction of sections 2 and 5
    The late-poll limit of the driver is 70 ms (HAK_RING_MAX_POLL_NS, since r404), not 20 ms as first written
    from the top of BUILD-NOTES.md. A start that takes 23-48 ms is therefore NOT a fault by itself; it uses up
    a third to two thirds of the allowance, and the fault comes when the host's stream creation takes longer
    than 70 ms or coincides with another hold-up. Sections 2.1, 2.3 and 5.3 are corrected in place.

6.1 Source and build
    Authoritative source: src/efi/audio_stream/driver/ (HakAudioStream.c and the files apply-driver-extension.py
    lists); build copy work/ha/OpenCorePkg/Staging/AudioDxe/HdaController/ (identical before and after this
    work); build: build-host-baseline.cmd work\charon-audio-driver-r347 (MSVC 14.51, EDK2 in work/ha).
    A rebuild of the unchanged source gave AudioDxe-r408.efi byte for byte (sha256 910ada69...).

6.3 The change (HakAudioStream.c:207-234; same text in work/ha)
    After HdaControllerSetStreamState(TRUE) returns the driver reads the cursor once. If it is 0, LastTick and
    LastProgressTick are taken there; a cursor that has already moved keeps the clock taken before the call.
    The 70 ms and 200 ms limits and every later poll are unchanged; on a card whose start takes microseconds the
    clock moves by microseconds. Trace builds print the start time, the cursor and the first three polls.
    Test builds only: -DHAK_AUDIO_STARTDELAY_MS=n (a Stall before the RUN write) and -DHAK_AUDIO_OLDSTART=1.
    Builds: work/charon-audio-driver-r409/AudioDxe-r409.efi (208896 bytes, sha256

          656 pumps, without a new request;
        70 stalls with sound in between in a row: 70 immediate restarts, no error (more than the old limit of 64);
        two stalls back to back, then sound: two restarts, no rest; Stop and Shutdown during a rest succeed.
      The same test against the provider before this work (host-provider-stall-test-before-r409.txt): 64 restarts,
      2936 pump errors, speech refused from then on, 0 frames after the card plays again.

6.9 Recommendation
    Nothing measured is worse with AudioDxe-r409 than with r408, nothing was unstable with it that was stable
    with r408, and it removes the measured trigger (6.2, 6.5). On an idle host the two cannot be told apart
    (6.4): the gain shows only when the host's stream creation takes more than 70 ms, and that was shown with a
    model, not with a real slow host. By the coordinator's rule r409 may ship. The one thing against it lies
    outside this work: r409 has not run on a physical card; the change there is one extra read of the cursor
    and a clock taken some microseconds later. If the release cannot have a hardware pass, keeping AudioDxe-r408
    with the StallPolicy loses nothing that was observed today: the StallPolicy alone turns the storm into
    rests and a working voice (4.2); r409 additionally keeps the speech (6.5).
